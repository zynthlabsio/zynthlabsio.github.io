Fixed price vs hourly: which is cheaper for small tasks?

Buying services · 17 November 2026

Hourly billing feels fair — you pay for the time, nothing more. For small, well-defined tasks it often costs more than a fixed price, and the reason is simple: with hourly billing, you carry the risk.

Where hourly billing quietly adds up

  • Speed risk is yours. A slow worker, a learning curve, a tool problem — every extra hour is on your invoice.
  • The total is unknown until the end. You approve an estimate, not a price, and estimates drift upward far more often than down.
  • You pay for coordination. Status questions, clarifications and revisions are all billable time.

What a fixed price changes

A flat price per task moves those risks to the seller. The quote is the total, agreed before work starts. If the task takes longer than expected, that is the seller's problem — which is also why sellers work to define the task sharply before quoting.

When fixed price is the better buy

  • The output can be described exactly: a brief, a spreadsheet, a post.
  • “Done” can be checked when it arrives.
  • The task is small enough that one quote covers it comfortably.

When hourly is fairer

Open-ended work — exploring a question nobody has scoped yet, ongoing assistance, tasks whose size depends on what you find — cannot be honestly priced flat. A fixed quote there either hides a large safety margin or invites corner-cutting. Pay hourly, with a cap and regular check-ins.

The practical test

Ask one question before ordering: can I write down exactly what I will receive, in one or two sentences? If yes, a fixed price will usually be cheaper and always be more predictable. If no, scope the task first — or accept hourly billing with your eyes open.
